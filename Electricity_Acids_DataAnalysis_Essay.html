📝 Data Analysis + Essay Question
(a) Explain why the bulbs in series are dimmer than the bulbs in parallel.
(b) Use the data to calculate the resistance of ONE bulb.
(c) Calculate the total resistance of the series circuit and the parallel circuit.
(d) Why is the total current in the parallel circuit (0.90A) exactly three times that of a single bulb (0.30A)?
(e) Essay: Compare the advantages and disadvantages of series and parallel circuits in household wiring. Use scientific principles to support your answer.
✅ Model Answer — V-C-D-C + J-E-A-L
(a) Brightness difference:
In series, voltage is shared: 6V ÷ 3 = 2V per bulb. Each bulb only gets 2V, so it's dim.
In parallel, each bulb gets the full 6V — same as the single bulb — so they all shine at normal brightness.

(b) Resistance of one bulb: R = V/I = 6V ÷ 0.30A = 20 Ω.

(c) Total resistance:
Series: Rtotal = 20 + 20 + 20 = 60 Ω (V=6V, I=0.10A → R=6÷0.10=60Ω ✓)
Parallel: 1/Rtotal = 1/20 + 1/20 + 1/20 → Rtotal = 6.67 Ω (V=6V, I=0.90A → R=6÷0.90=6.67Ω ✓)

(d) Why 3× current? In parallel, each bulb draws 0.30A independently. Total current = 0.30 + 0.30 + 0.30 = 0.90A. Parallel branches are independent — adding more bulbs doesn't reduce voltage per bulb.

(e) Essay — Series vs Parallel in Household Wiring:
Household circuits use PARALLEL wiring. This is because:
① Each appliance gets the full mains voltage (220V), ensuring normal operation.
② Appliances can be switched on/off independently — one bulb blowing doesn't affect others.
③ Adding more appliances reduces total resistance and increases total current — this is why overloading is a risk.

Series circuits are not suitable for households because: voltage is shared (dim lights), one break stops everything (e.g. old Christmas tree lights), and all devices must be on simultaneously.

(d) Essay — Two other factors affecting resistance:

① Cross-sectional area (thickness): Thicker wires have lower resistance. This is because a thicker wire provides more space for electrons to flow — like a wide pipe allows more water to flow than a narrow pipe. Doubling the cross-sectional area halves the resistance.

② Temperature: For most metals, increasing temperature increases resistance. This is because higher temperature causes metal atoms to vibrate more, making it harder for electrons to pass through the lattice — more collisions between electrons and vibrating atoms.

Formula summary: R ∝ L (length), R ∝ 1/A (cross-sectional area), and R increases with temperature (for metals).

A student connects a wire directly across the terminals of a battery (bypassing the bulb). The wire becomes very hot.

(a) Explain, using the concept of resistance, why the wire gets hot.

(c) Why is it dangerous to touch a bare live wire in a household circuit?
為甚麼觸摸家用電路中的裸露火線是危險的？
🔤 Key Terms 關鍵詞： short circuit 短路 · bypass 繞過 · heating effect of current 電流的熱效應 · fuse 保險絲 · earth wire / grounding 接地線 · live wire 火線/帶電線 · neutral wire 中性線 · electric shock 觸電 · overloading 過載 · circuit breaker 斷路器
✅ Model Answer — J-E-A-L for (b)
(a) Why the wire gets hot:
When a wire is connected directly across the battery (short circuit), the current bypasses the bulb (which had resistance). The wire itself has very low resistance. According to I = V/R, when R is very small, the current becomes very large. This large current causes the wire to heat up rapidly (heating effect of current: P = I²R).

(b) Safety devices:
① Fuse: A thin wire that melts and breaks the circuit if current exceeds a safe value. It is connected in series with the live wire. If a fault causes high current, the fuse melts first, cutting off the supply and preventing fire.
② Earth wire (grounding): A low-resistance wire connected to the metal casing of an appliance. If the live wire touches the metal casing, current flows through the earth wire (not through the user), triggering a large current that blows the fuse and cuts off the supply.

(c) Danger of touching a live wire: The human body provides a path to earth. Current flows through the body, causing electric shock. Household voltage (220V) can deliver a fatal current > 30 mA.

✅ Model Answer
Formulas: P = VI (Power = Voltage × Current), E = P × t (Energy = Power × Time)

(a) Daily energy:
Refrigerator: 0.15 kW × 24 h = 3.6 kWh
AC: 2.0 kW × 6 h = 12.0 kWh

② Turn off appliances when not in use: Reducing operating time t directly reduces energy E = P × t. Standby mode still consumes power — unplugging eliminates this "phantom load".
📝 Experiment Evaluation Question — J-E-A-L
A student wants to investigate whether the length of a wire affects its resistance. He uses the following setup:
• A 3V battery, an ammeter, a voltmeter, and connecting wires
• He tests wires of length 20cm, 40cm, 60cm, 80cm, and 100cm
• He uses wires of DIFFERENT thicknesses (some thin, some thick)
• He only does each measurement ONCE

Evaluate the experimental design. Identify TWO problems and suggest improvements.
✅ Model Answer — J-E-A-L
J — Judgment: The experiment has significant flaws — the results would be unreliable.

E — Evidence of problems:
Problem ① — Multiple variables changing: The student used wires of different thicknesses. Thickness also affects resistance (R ∝ 1/A). If both length and thickness vary, we cannot tell which factor caused the change in resistance. Only ONE variable should change at a time (fair test).
Problem ② — No repeats: Each length was only measured once. The result might be affected by random errors (e.g. poor connection, reading error). Without repeats, we cannot calculate an average or identify anomalous results.

A — Adjustment (improvements):
① Use wires of the same material and same thickness — only vary the length.
② Repeat each measurement at least 3 times and calculate the average current/resistance.

L — Limitations: Even after improvements, the experiment doesn't account for temperature changes — longer wires under current may heat up more, increasing resistance. This could affect the accuracy of the conclusion.

📝 Data Analysis Question
(a) What is the independent variable and dependent variable?
(b) At what volume of acid does neutralization occur? How do you know?
(c) Describe the pH change pattern. What is special about the region near 48-50 mL?
(d) Write the chemical equation for the neutralization reaction.
(e) Essay: Explain why the pH changes slowly at first, then drops rapidly near the equivalence point. Use the concept of H⁺ and OH⁻ ions.
✅ Model Answer — V-C-D-C
V — Variables: IV = Volume of acid added; DV = pH (measured by universal indicator / pH meter); CV = Same concentration (0.1M), same volume of NaOH (50mL), room temperature.

D — Data analysis:
(a) IV = Volume of acid added (mL); DV = pH.
(b) Neutralization occurs at approximately 48 mL — this is where pH = 7 (neutral). The indicator turns green at this point.
(c) The pH changes slowly from 13→9 (0-45mL), then drops sharply from 9→3 between 45-52mL (only 7mL span), then levels off at pH 2. The sharp drop near 48mL is called the equivalence point.
(d) HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (acid + alkali → salt + water)

(e) Essay — Why the pH drops slowly then sharply:
Before neutralization (0-45mL): Excess OH⁻ ions from NaOH dominate. Adding H⁺ ions neutralizes some OH⁻, but there are still plenty of OH⁻ ions left, so pH stays high (alkaline). The pH changes slowly because the solution is buffered by excess OH⁻.

Near equivalence point (45-50mL): Most OH⁻ ions have been neutralized. Adding just a few more mL of acid causes a dramatic change — the remaining OH⁻ is used up, and suddenly H⁺ ions are in excess. This is why pH drops from 9 to 3 within 5 mL — the "pH jump".

After neutralization (>50mL): Excess H⁺ ions dominate, pH stays around 2 (strongly acidic).
🔬 Experiment 5 — Neutralization: Temperature Change 中和反應的溫度變化 中英對照

🔤 Key Terms 關鍵詞： exothermic reaction 放熱反應 · temperature change 溫度變化 · maximum temperature 最高溫度 · heat released 釋放的熱量 · neutralisation is exothermic 中和作用是放熱的

📝 Data Analysis Question
(a) Why does the temperature rise when acid and alkali are mixed?
(b) At what volume does the temperature peak? What does this tell you?
(c) The concentrations are equal (both 0.5M). What volume ratio gives complete neutralization? What is the mole ratio?
(d) Why does the temperature decrease after the peak?
(e) Essay: Explain the relationship between this temperature change experiment and the pH change experiment (Experiment 4). How do they support each other's conclusions?
✅ Model Answer — V-C-D-C
V — Variables: IV = Volume of HCl added; DV = Temperature; CV = Same concentration (0.5M), same initial volume of NaOH (25mL), same container.

D — Data analysis:
(a) Neutralization is an exothermic reaction — H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O releases heat energy, which raises the temperature of the solution.
(b) Temperature peaks at 25 mL of HCl (32.5°C). This is the equivalence point — all OH⁻ has been neutralized by H⁺, maximum heat released.
(c) Volume ratio = 25 mL HCl : 25 mL NaOH = 1:1. Since both are 0.5M, the mole ratio is also 1:1 — HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O.
(d) After the peak, adding more acid dilutes the solution and adds cooler acid at room temperature. The reaction is complete, so no more heat is produced — the excess acid cools the mixture toward room temperature.

(e) Essay — How the two experiments support each other:
In Experiment 4, pH changed sharply near 48 mL (0.1M concentrations). In this experiment, temperature peaks at 25 mL (0.5M concentrations). Both support the same 1:1 mole ratio: the equivalence point is when equal moles of acid and alkali react.

The pH change tells us when neutralization is complete (sudden pH drop), while the temperature change tells us how much energy is released. Together, they provide strong evidence that neutralization involves H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O in a 1:1 ratio, and that the reaction is exothermic.

(d) Design a simple experiment to test whether sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) is a strong acid. Describe the expected results and explain your reasoning.
設計簡單實驗來測試硫酸是否為強酸，描述預期結果並解釋推理。
✅ Model Answer — V-C-D-C + J-E-A-L
(a) Lower pH: HCl has pH 1.0, which is much lower than ethanoic acid (pH 2.9). This is because HCl is a strong acid — it fully dissociates into H⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in water, producing many H⁺ ions. Ethanoic acid is a weak acid — it only partially dissociates, producing far fewer H⁺ ions at the same concentration.

(b) Brightness difference: Conductivity depends on the number of mobile ions in solution. HCl fully dissociates → lots of H⁺ and Cl⁻ ions → high conductivity → bright bulb. Ethanoic acid only partially dissociates → fewer ions → low conductivity → dim bulb.

(c) Essay — Strong vs Concentrated:
"Strong acid" refers to the degree of dissociation (how completely the acid splits into ions). HCl is strong — virtually all HCl molecules dissociate. Ethanoic acid is weak — only about 1% dissociates.

"Concentrated acid" refers to the amount of acid dissolved in a given volume of water. You can have concentrated weak acid (e.g. concentrated ethanoic acid — vinegar) or dilute strong acid (e.g. 0.01M HCl — dilute but still fully dissociated).

(d) Experiment to test if H₂SO₄ is strong:
Method: Prepare 0.1M H₂SO₄ and measure its pH using a pH meter. Compare with 0.1M HCl (known strong acid, pH ≈ 1.0).
Expected result: If H₂SO₄ is strong, pH should be ≈ 1.0 (or slightly lower since H₂SO₄ releases 2 H⁺ per molecule). If pH is closer to 3, it's weak.
Cross-check: Test conductivity — strong acid gives bright bulb; weak acid gives dim bulb.

A student has three unlabelled solutions: A (pH 2), B (pH 7), and C (pH 11).

(a) Using ONLY red litmus paper, explain how the student could identify all three solutions.

(b) Using ONLY universal indicator solution, explain how the student could identify all three.

(c) Evaluate 評價： Which method (litmus or universal indicator) gives more information? Explain your answer with reference to the pH scale.

(d) Real-world application 實際應用： Farmers sometimes add lime (calcium oxide) to acidic soil. Explain the scientific principle behind this practice.
農民有時會加石灰（氧化鈣）到酸性土壤中。解釋這做法背後的科學原理。
✅ Model Answer — J-E-A-L for (c)
(a) Using red litmus:
Red litmus turns blue in alkali and stays red in acid/neutral.
① Test solution C: red litmus → blue → C is alkali.
② Test A and B: both stay red → cannot distinguish acid from neutral using ONLY red litmus. (If blue litmus were also available, A would turn it red while B would not.)

(b) Using universal indicator:
Universal indicator gives different colours at different pH levels:
Solution A (pH 2) → red (strong acid)
Solution B (pH 7) → green (neutral)
Solution C (pH 11) → purple/blue (alkali)
All three can be identified with one test each.

(c) Evaluation — J-E-A-L:
J: Universal indicator gives more information than litmus.
E: Litmus only tells you if a solution is acidic (red) or alkaline (blue) — it cannot distinguish strength (e.g. pH 2 vs pH 5 both turn red). Universal indicator gives a range of colours that correspond to specific pH values, so you can estimate the actual pH.
A: For best accuracy, use a pH meter — it gives a numerical reading rather than colour comparison.

(d) Farmers adding lime:
Lime (calcium oxide, CaO) is a base. When added to acidic soil, it undergoes neutralization: CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (lime reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide), then Ca(OH)₂ + 2H⁺ → Ca²⁺ + 2H₂O — the OH⁻ ions from lime neutralize the H⁺ ions in the acidic soil, raising the pH to a level suitable for crop growth.
📝 Essay Question — Neutralization in Daily Life
Neutralization reactions are widely used in everyday life.

(a) Explain why antacid tablets (containing magnesium hydroxide) can relieve indigestion. Write the chemical equation.
(b) Bee stings are acidic (contain methanoic acid). Wasp stings are alkaline. Explain why different treatments are needed for bee stings vs wasp stings.
(c) Experiment evaluation: A student wants to test which antacid brand is most effective. He drops antacid tablets into 50 mL of 0.1M HCl and measures the time for the solution to become neutral (pH 7). Identify TWO factors he should control, and explain why.
✅ Model Answer — J-E-A-L for (c)
(a) Antacid mechanism:
Indigestion is caused by excess stomach acid (HCl). Antacids contain bases like magnesium hydroxide, Mg(OH)₂, which neutralize the excess acid:
Mg(OH)₂ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H₂O
This reduces the acidity in the stomach, relieving the discomfort.

(b) Bee vs wasp stings:
Bee sting contains acid (methanoic acid) → treat with a weak alkali like baking soda (NaHCO₃) to neutralize it.
Wasp sting contains alkali → treat with a weak acid like vinegar (ethanoic acid) to neutralize it.
Using the wrong treatment would worsen the sting — e.g. putting alkali on an alkali sting!

(c) Experiment evaluation — J-E-A-L:
J: The design is reasonable but needs strict control of variables.
E: The student must control:
① Size/mass of tablet: Different brands may have different tablet sizes. Use the same mass of each tablet to ensure fair comparison.
② Temperature: Reaction rate increases with temperature. All trials must be at the same temperature (e.g. room temp 25°C).
③ Concentration and volume of HCl: Use exactly 50 mL of 0.1M HCl for every test.
A: Repeat each test 3 times and calculate the average time. Use a pH meter for accurate neutralization detection rather than indicator colour (which is subjective).
L: This experiment measures speed of neutralization, not total neutralizing capacity. A fast but weak antacid might appear better than a slow but strong one.
